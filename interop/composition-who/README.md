# DPS action binding against the WHO slot of draft-mih-sato-agent-accountability-composition, section 6.2, read against the -02 candidate (commit f79f9d1)

Date: 29 October 2026 against -01; revised 31 October 2026 against the -02 candidate, at the request of Iman Schrock (co-author of -02). Author of this reading: Laurent Lemonnier (contributor of the four DPS extensions). Prepared with AI assistance; reading not yet reviewed by the draft's authors. The -01 reading is at commit d37ac81 of this repository; see "Changes in this reading" at the end.

## What this table compares

- On the DPS side: the action binding extension 0.3.0 (`extensions/action-binding/`) and the signatures extension 0.2.0 (`extensions/signatures/`) of the Decision Provenance Standard, at commit `007f8c9` of the repository's `main` branch (28 October 2026, merge of PR #92), read in a clean worktree. The trust policy and key states they use are anchoring 0.2.0's, by reference.
- On the draft side: section 6.2 "The WHO Slot: Named-Human Authorization" of the -02 candidate of draft-mih-sato-agent-accountability-composition (five authors: Mih, Sato, Schrock, Bu, Sokolov), at commit `f79f9d1d652151ab8fa55485d97331be023692c7` of `agent-accountability/agent-accountability-composition` (archive at that commit, SHA-256 `918fa90d12527191…`; its `SHA256SUMS-02.txt` verified), with sections 4.1 and 4.2 for the subject digest and the digest context, and sections 8 and 8.1 for conformance. Read against the posted -01 (August 2026): section 6.2 differs in one paragraph, the commitment rule (row A-11); sections 4.3.1, 6.1.4, 8.1.1, 8.1.5, 8.1.7, 10 and 12 also changed and are read below where they bear on this table; the producer and verifier lists, the negative classes, sections 4.1, 4.2 and 8.1.3 are unchanged.
- The candidate WHO record is the action-binding attachment (one per decision record) together with the person's signature over its outcome stage, carried in a signatures attachment beside it. The decision record itself (options, reasons, authority) is not the WHO record; section 6.2 places that question above the composition. See "The layer above".

What this table does not claim:

- The DPS is not a registered WHO profile and has not been run against the shared composition vector of section 8. Nothing here is a conformance claim.
- The table is the reading of the author of the two extensions, not of the draft's authors. Where the two texts use different words, the DPS words are quoted and the draft's are not reinterpreted.
- In the DPS's own words, "the records and the attachment remain input, not evidence" (action binding README, "Scope"). A verified signature shows who issued or signed exact bytes. It does not show that the claims are true, that the person read what they signed, or that the action was right.

Every field, rule and case cited below exists in the files named. Rule numbers are the numbered items of the action binding README ("The attachment", items 1 to 13). Both test runners were run on this commit with `/root/claude-ops/dps/venv/bin/python`: action binding, 359 cases, all as expected, 4.5 s; signatures, 418 cases, all as expected, 6.0 s.

Status words: **meets** (the requirement is carried by a field or rule, with a case), **meets with note** (carried, with a difference worth stating), **gap** (not carried).

## Table C. VERIFIED versus ACCEPTED

Section 6.2: VERIFIED is "the signature(s) and the digest binding hold, given a public key; objective and offline"; ACCEPTED is "the relying party additionally trusts the authorizing principal(s) via out-of-band key pinning; a relying-party decision". Neither "implies the authorization was sufficient for the action".

| Draft claim | DPS carrier | How the DPS keeps them apart |
|---|---|---|
| VERIFIED | `person_signature_result.status` = `verified` when `signature`, `subject` and `key_state` are (signatures README, "The assertion is verified only when `signature`, `subject` and `key_state` are. Identity is not part of it"). `signature` names `key_ref`, `algorithm`, `reference_source: trust_policy`. | The check is offline: the verifier builds the signed bytes and verifies with the key the policy gives for `key_ref`. One difference from "given a public key": the DPS never takes a key from the receipt or the attachment (signatures cases A07, B22, P07 refused; verifier rule 1, "MUST take every source of trust from its own trust policy"). The key must be listed in the policy, in role `person`. Listing a key is configuration; it is not yet acceptance of the person (next row). |
| ACCEPTED | `identity` dimension: `basis: trust_policy`, `source_ref` (the policy's `identity_source_ref` for the key), `holder` (the policy's), `issuer_named` (the outcome's `actor_ref`), `consistency`. Verified only when the signature and the key's state are, when the policy names a source for the key, and when the holder is the actor the issuer names. | A signature can be verified while identity is `failed` and `inconsistent` (N31: the issuer names another actor than the key's holder; N45) or `failed` for want of an identity source (N69), or `not_checked` when the key's state is not verified (N53, P33, P34). "It never rests on the signature, on the issuer's statement or on an authenticator flag" (verifier rule 4; P04, P05, J11). The relying party's own decision is outside the report: the report states the policy's binding and its source. |
| Neither implies sufficiency | Action binding report: `authority_assurance` ("the authority an approval asserts", verified only against an independent source such as the permission system rechecked: P09, P10), apart from `identity_assurance` and `issuer_signature` ("Separate assertions": "An organisation's seal grants no authority"). | Sufficiency ("was this authorization sufficient for this action") is not a dimension of either report. The `scope_of_conclusion` lists `organisational_authority` and `legal_effect` among what is never assessed (signatures) and `legal_requirements`, `conformance_level` (action binding). |

## Gaps and open points

1. **Quorum (M-of-N, ordered).** Gap by design in action binding 0.3.0: "It sets no rule on how many people review an action". Several approvals of one specification are recorded as separate outcomes, each with its own core backing (L64), and the verifier checks that no core field backs two outcomes. There is no threshold descriptor, no eligible-signer list and no order among signers. A quorum would be a new stage kind or a new extension; nothing in the current text promises one.
2. **Freshness and validity window of a single approval.** Answered, with a remaining gap on the verifier side. `person_approval` signs `signed_at`, the record, stage, outcome and digests, and carries no nonce. Replay is prevented by what the signature names, not by a one-time token. Issue #93 of the Standard asked the Steward whether a designating-policy digest and a short validity should be signed inside the person's approval. His answer (29 October 2026): neither needs a new member, because both can sit under the person's signature through the specification the approval signs. The policy that designates the approver is the issuer's document named by `policy_version`, written as the SHA-256 of its bytes; a deadline for one approval is a member of the specification the deployer declares material; the executor's recheck before each attempt refuses an approval that is too old, on its own clock. Signatures stays 0.2.0 and action binding 0.3.0. What remains: no reference check compares the approval's time with the attempt's time (B-5), and the deadline is a deployer's member, not a field the profile names. A standing approval keeps its period inside its signed bounds; the difference between the two is intended.
3. **Where the one-time semantics are signed.** For a single approval, `request_limit` is a member of the issuer's outcome stage, under the issuer's seal (`issuer_signature`), not under the person's signed bytes. For a standing approval, `max_requests` and the period are inside the bounds whose digest the person signs. A relying party that wants the person to have signed the count has the second form today.
4. **Digest context is declared by the profile, not carried per record.** SHA-256, lowercase hex in fields, raw 32 bytes in the signed preimage, no domain separation for `spec_digest`, `"DPS-SIG" 0x00` plus profile, version and type for every signature. A WHO profile registration would state this once; the attachment carries only `profile` and `profile_version`.
5. **No selective disclosure, now a stated rule.** `actor_ref`, `reviewer_ref` and `key_ref` are in clear; a commitment to the principal is not defined (A-11, -02 section 6.2). The -02 candidate's section 12 adds that profiles MUST distinguish the native evidence given to authorised verifiers from the public registration projection, and define the public fields, the disclosure policy, the correlation scope and the integrity-protected binding between the two. On the DPS side the native evidence is the export (portable verification) and the public projection is what draft-lemonnier-scitt-decision-provenance registers: digests only (the record's seal, the attachment's SHA-256, under CPB), bound to the native bytes by being their digests; the disclosure policy and the correlation scope are written nowhere as such. Two sentences to write, after IETF 127, either in the Standard or in the -02 of the decision-provenance draft.
6. **Times are the issuer's.** Every stage time is the issuer's statement and the report says so (`stage_order` has a time basis, P19). Independent time bounds the attachment from above only (`no_later_than`). See "The ordering class".
7. **The public key never travels with the receipt.** The DPS refuses a key or a certificate carried in an attachment (A07, A08, X26). A verifier that wants the draft's "given a public key" check places the key in its own trust policy first. The identity binding is then a separate dimension (Table C).
8. **Result vocabulary.** The DPS uses `verified`, `failed`, `not_checked`, `unavailable`, `not_supported`; section 8.1.3 uses pass, fail, not_evaluated, unsupported, indeterminate. The -02 candidate (section 8.1.5) draws the last three apart: unsupported for an unrecognised required profile, indeterminate for readable inputs whose comparison cannot be resolved, not_evaluated for a required input absent from the bundle (its new vector COMP-JOIN-03). On that reading the DPS words map one to one: `not_supported` to unsupported; `unavailable` (bytes the attachment declares unavailable) to not_evaluated; `not_checked` to not_evaluated when an input is missing (an unsigned attachment, G04) and to indeterminate when the inputs are there and the check cannot conclude (a subject the verifier cannot recompute). The mapping would still be stated once in a conformance pack. Both sides agree that only the first value is a pass and that the others are never upgraded.
9. **The person's signature does not observe the effect.** This is consistent with section 6.2's exclusion, and it is why the DPS relies on the attachment for ordering. See below.

## The ordering class

Section 6.2 closes "a post-hoc ratification presented as pre-execution authorization" only with "a source that observed both terms, or ... two records whose producing boundaries can be related in a way that orders the two terms". An artefact produced on the authorising side alone cannot close it.

In the DPS the attachment is produced on the execution side, by the issuer who runs the action, and it records both terms as dated stages in one document: the review (what was shown, `shown_spec_digest`), the outcome (`approved_spec_digest`), the request (`authorised_by`), the recheck (`result: authorised`, `authority_source_ref`), the attempt (`recheck_ref`) and the observed result (`observed`, `basis`). In the fixture A0 these are v1 09:10, o1 09:12, q1 09:15, c1 09:16, x1 09:16:05, r1 09:16:09 on 1 October 2026. The verifier checks "that each stage comes after the stages it names", that each attempt follows an authorising recheck, and that no request rests on a rejection or an escalation (L17, L18, L26, L19). The ordering input therefore comes from the boundary that produced the effect term, which is the criterion the draft states. The person's own signature (`person_approval`) carries `signed_at` and nothing about the effect; the DPS does not present it as closing the class. The -02 candidate's section 4.3.1 records that the principal-binding framework it reviewed (Bu, now -06) asks for evidence of pre-effect authorisation ordering and of the binding between an attester and the named principal: this paragraph is the DPS's answer to the first, with its stated limit below; A-1 and Table C are its answer to the second (identity from the verifier's policy, never from the issuer).

What this does not prove. The stage times are the issuer's, and the runner states the limit of L17: "An issuer that backdates every stage consistently is not detected." The only independent time is a timestamp over the issuer's signature value, read beside the attachment: it shows that the attachment's bytes, and so every stage they record, existed no later than T (`time_assurance.no_later_than`), and a stage dated after T is reported as a finding (G39). It gives an upper bound on the whole document, not an order between approval and effect. An independent timestamp over the person's signature (T01, N54) bounds the approval from above as well; nothing in the DPS bounds the effect from below, so the two records cannot be related into a strict order by independent time alone. A validly signed older attachment with fewer stages verifies too (G03): the signature shows what the issuer issued, not that nothing was issued after. Finally, `recheck` and `result` are the issuer's records of what the permission system and the target answered; the verifier that needs more "asks the permission system itself" (README, "Separate assertions").

## The layer above

The decision record of the Standard (options, reasons, the authority asserted, the review log and the affirmation) is not a WHO record and does not claim to be one; it answers the question section 6.2 places "a layer above the composition", whether this authorisation was sufficient for this action. The attachment cites it by `decision_id`, and the record is cited across a boundary by its seal (`seal_hash`, the SHA-256 of its covered bytes), as the interop cases do with a typed reference. An EMILIA authorization receipt could be cited from a record's `affirmation_record.method` or from an outcome's `method`, `identity_evidence_ref` or `legal_signature_ref` as evidence of the approval of that record; no DPS check reads such a reference today, so it would be carried as the issuer's statement until a profile says how to verify it. For a plan approved as a whole and executed as steps, that layer is built in `plan-run/` of this repository: one approval, N capsules, a manifest between them, and the joins that say which capsule is which step.
