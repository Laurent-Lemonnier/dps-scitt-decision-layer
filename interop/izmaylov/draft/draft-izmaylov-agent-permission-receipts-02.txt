      Signed Permissions and Action Receipts for Automated Agents
              draft-izmaylov-agent-permission-receipts-02

Abstract

   This document specifies records with which a third party can
   establish, later and without network access, what an automated agent,
   such as an artificial intelligence (AI) agent, was permitted to do
   and what it recorded doing.  The person for whom the agent acts, the
   principal, signs a permission with a passkey; the permission carries
   limits that a verifier can check mechanically.  The agent signs a
   receipt for each action with Ed25519 and the Module-Lattice-Based
   Digital Signature Algorithm (ML-DSA-87).  Receipts are chained and
   entered in a log whose signed tree heads are time-stamped.  A
   verifier reports, separately, whether the records are sound and
   whether each recorded action stayed within its permission.  The
   content of each record is specified independently of its encoding;
   one encoding, JSON Web Signature (JWS) over canonical JavaScript
   Object Notation (JSON), is defined.

1.  Introduction

   A principal signs a permission for an agent: which actions, with
   which services, within which limits, from when until when.  The agent
   signs a receipt for each action it takes; a service may countersign
   it.  A recorder appends receipts to a log and periodically signs its
   tree head, which a Time-Stamping Authority (TSA) time-stamps.  Later,
   with no network access, a verifier checks the records and answers
   three separate questions: whether the records are sound, whether
   every signature could be verified on that device, and whether each
   recorded action stayed within its permission.












Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                   [Page 3]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


   This document specifies the records and the verifier's rules:
   evidence, not enforcement.  Nothing in this document stops an action
   when it is attempted, and an action for which no receipt was written
   leaves no trace in the agent's own log (Section 18).  It names the
   role of an enforcement point that refuses an action before it takes
   effect, an executor (Section 2), but does not specify how an executor
   decides or keeps its records, how records are carried, how keys and
   trust anchors are distributed, or what to do about a finding
   (Section 15).

   When an automated agent acts for a person, the questions asked
   afterward are the same: was the action authorized, by whom, within
   what limits, did the other party agree, and when?  Authorization
   protocols answer at the moment of a request, typically with tokens
   that are not kept as evidence.  This document specifies records that
   a third party can verify later, without network access.  Each record
   is signed by the party that made it.  Whoever keeps the log can still
   leave out or delay entries; Section 18 states how far that reaches.

   The content model (Section 4 through Section 10) says what each
   record holds, who signs it, and how a verifier judges it, apart from
   how records are written as bytes.  This document defines one encoding
   (Section 11): JSON Web Signature (JWS) [RFC7515] over JavaScript
   Object Notation (JSON) [RFC8259] in one canonical form [RFC8785];
   Section 12 states what another encoding, such as one with Concise
   Binary Object Representation (CBOR) Object Signing and Encryption
   (COSE), has to define.  Logs use the Merkle tree of [RFC9162], and
   time-stamps come from a TSA [RFC3161].  Agents sign with Ed25519
   [RFC8032] and the Module-Lattice-Based Digital Signature Algorithm
   ML-DSA-87 [FIPS204] side by side; a signed tree head adds the
   Stateless Hash-Based Digital Signature Algorithm SLH-DSA [FIPS205].

   Other drafts (Section 13) already have principal-signed
   authorizations, signed and hash-chained receipts for each action,
   passkey approval of one exact action, signed budgets enforced when an
   action is attempted, a cumulative limit checked afterward against the
   amounts that a decision point recorded, and delegation that only
   narrows.  This document adds three things.  First, the permission
   that the principal signs with a passkey [WebAuthn] carries limits
   that a verifier can check mechanically (totals, single amounts,
   counts, and totals or counts within a rolling period) and conditions
   (the other party's countersignature, or the principal's approval of
   one action).  Second, a cancellation can be acknowledged by the
   agent.  Third, it specifies a verifier that adds up the signed
   receipts themselves and says, from the records alone and after the
   fact, whether each recorded action, including a helper agent's,
   stayed within the permission.

   The records are evidence, not a verdict: a sound record can show an
   agent acting outside its permission.  This document is the core of a
   published format description [FORMAT]; Section 15 lists the parts
   specified only there.

2.  Conventions and Terminology

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
   BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
   capitals, as shown here.

   The identifiers that [FORMAT] uses in labels and member names are
   given in parentheses after each term.

   Principal:  The person who signs a permission with a passkey
      ("issuer").
   Passkey:  A credential [WebAuthn] in a device, unlocked by user
      verification such as a biometric or a personal identification
      number (PIN).
   Agent:  Software that acts for the principal.  It deals with
      services, which may countersign its receipts.
   Helper agent, or helper:  An agent that acts under a delegation
      (Section 7).
   Service:  The other party to an action, named in a permission's
      "with"; it may countersign (Section 6).
   Executor:  Software, other than the agent, that takes actions for the
      agent or holds what they need, such as a credential, and refuses
      an action that its check of the permission does not allow;
      [FORMAT] specifies one such check (Section 15).  A permission
      names an executor in "with", as it names a service; to
      countersign, the executor needs its key set there (Section 6).
   Permission:  The principal's signed statement of which agent may do
      what, within which limits, with whom, and when ("slip").
   Action receipt, or receipt:  The agent's signed statement that it
      took one action ("stub").  It is not a Receipt in the sense of
      Supply Chain Integrity, Transparency, and Trust (SCITT) [RFC9943]
      (Section 13).
   Delegation:  An agent's signed hand-over of part of its permission
      ("pass").
   Record:  A signed statement of one of the eight kinds of Section 4.1.
   Log:  A sequence of entries, only ever added to ("book").
   Entry:  One record together with what is carried beside it, as one
      element of a log (Section 9.1).
   Recorder:  Whoever keeps a log.
   Signed tree head, or head:  The recorder's signed statement of a
      log's size and root ("seal").



Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                   [Page 5]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


   Chain:  The receipts of one agent under one permission or one
      delegation, each naming the one before (Section 6).
   Verifier:  Anyone, or any software, that verifies records
      ("checker").
   Core verifier:  A verifier that implements this document and none of
      the parts specified only in [FORMAT] (Section 15).
   Digest:  The SHA-256 hash [FIPS180-4] of some bytes.
   Encoding:  How records, keys, and logs are written as bytes and how
      signatures are carried (Section 11, Section 12).
   Problem:  A reason why an entry, or the log, cannot be relied on.
   Sound:  Of an entry or a log: having no problem.
   Finding:  A statement that a sound receipt or delegation lies outside
      its permission.  A finding is not a problem.
   Compared:  Of a receipt or delegation: judged against its permission
      (Section 10.1).
   Counted:  Of a time-stamp: from a trusted TSA, on a record whose
      signatures were all verified (Section 9.3).
   Intact:  Of a log: no problem was found and everything was verified
      (Section 10.1).

   The base64url encoding is that of Section 5 of [RFC4648], without
   padding.  Where a rule is followed by a code in parentheses, such as
   "(chain-broken)", an entry that breaks the rule has a problem.  A
   verifier MUST report that code for it, unless it stopped at an
   earlier problem of the same entry (Section 10.1).  Every finding of a
   compared receipt or delegation is reported: each code once, except
   over-period-limit, which is reported once for each period limit
   exceeded.  Appendix A lists the codes.

3.  Overview and Example

   Once the principal has signed a permission, the agent signs a receipt
   for each action, naming the permission and the receipt before it.
   The recorder writes each receipt, with any countersignature and
   approval, as one log entry, and periodically appends a time-stamped
   signed tree head.  This example follows the demonstration published
   with [FORMAT]; the parties and orders are invented.  An office agent
   may order supplies up to 200 GBP in total and 100 GBP for one order,
   and send two messages in any hour.  Every order needs the supplier's
   countersignature, and an order above 60 GBP the principal's approval.
   The permission's content is shown in the JSON encoding (Section 11),
   with spaces and line breaks added, and values abbreviated:

    "id": "<base64url>",

      "origin": "https://sign.example.org", "rpId": "example.org"},

4.  Common Rules

   Section 4 through Section 10 are the content model; Section 11 is the
   JSON encoding.  Maps are written here as in JSON: {"unit", "value"}
   is a map with exactly those two members.

4.1.  Records and Signers

   A record is of one of eight kinds.  Each is signed by one party, with
   these methods, in this order; X is the identifier of the kind in the
   labels of [FORMAT]:

      +==================+==================+=======================+
      | Kind of record   | X                | Signed with           |
      +==================+==================+=======================+
      | Permission       | slip             | the principal's       |
      |                  |                  | passkey (Section 4.7) |
      +------------------+------------------+-----------------------+
      | Action receipt   | stub             | Ed25519, ML-DSA-87    |
      +------------------+------------------+-----------------------+
      | Countersignature | countersignature | Ed25519, ML-DSA-87    |
      +------------------+------------------+-----------------------+
      | Approval         | approval         | the principal's       |
      |                  |                  | passkey               |
      +------------------+------------------+-----------------------+
      | Delegation       | pass             | Ed25519, ML-DSA-87    |
      +------------------+------------------+-----------------------+
      | Cancellation     | cancellation     | the principal's       |

      | Acknowledgement  | acknowledgement  | Ed25519, ML-DSA-87    |

      | Signed tree head | seal             | Ed25519, ML-DSA-87,   |

   The content of a record holds a member "type" whose value is
   "provared.X.v0".  Every signature of a record covers its kind as well
   as its content, so that a signature made for one kind of record is
   not accepted as a signature on another; the encoding says how
   (Section 11.1).  The suffix "v0" marks this draft, so that a record
   of a later, frozen version is distinguished from a draft one.

4.2.  Content

   The content of a record is a map from member names, which are text,
   to values.  A value is a map of the same kind, a list of values,
   text, an integer from 0 to 2^53 - 1, or one of the values of
   Section 4.3.  There are no others: no true, false, or null, no
   fractions, and no negative numbers.  Text is a sequence of Unicode
   scalar values; its length is counted in code points.  The encoding
   writes the content in one form, which a verifier tests.

   "type" MUST match the kind of record that the encoding labels
   (payload-type-mismatch).  A map MUST hold exactly the members listed
   for it, each in its stated form (bad-field).

4.3.  Values

   Bytes:  A sequence of bytes, of the length stated for each member.
      Identifiers, digests, and the parts of keys are bytes.
   Identifier:  An "id" is 16 random bytes.  No two records in a log
      hold the same "id" (duplicate-id).
   Time:  A date and time in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), to the
      second, with no fraction and no offset.  It MUST denote a moment
      that exists: 29 February in a year that is not a leap year, an
      hour of 24, and a second of 60 are refused (bad-field).
   Digests:  A digest is 32 bytes.  A record's digest is that of its
      content, in the bytes that the encoding writes and that its
      signatures cover; it covers no signature.  Records name one
      another by it.  A "sha256" member holds a document's digest:
      records never hold documents.
   Name:  Text of 1 to 200 code points, chosen by the writer.  Except
      for the reserved names of documents (Section 6), a name is not
      checked against anything.
   Amount:  Exactly {"unit", "value"}, where "value" is an integer and
      the unit is 1 to 16 ASCII letters, digits, ".", "-", or "_",
      beginning with a letter or digit.

4.4.  Keys

   A key is a public key of one of these methods, and of no other (bad-
   key):




Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                   [Page 9]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


   Ed25519:           32 bytes ([RFC8032]).
   ML-DSA-87:         2,592 bytes ([FIPS204]).
   SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s:  64 bytes ([FIPS205]).
   ES256:             A P-256 public key, two coordinates of 32 bytes
                      (Section 3.4 of [RFC7518]).
   RS256:             An RSA modulus of 2,048 to 8,192 bits, with the
                      public exponent 65,537 (Section 3.3 of [RFC7518]).

   The key set of an agent or a service is exactly one Ed25519 key
   followed by one ML-DSA-87 key.  A recorder's key set adds one SLH-
   DSA-SHA2-256s key.  A principal's passkey is one ES256, RS256, or
   Ed25519 key.

   An Ed25519 key, wherever it appears, MUST NOT encode a y-coordinate
   greater than or equal to p = 2^255 - 19, and MUST NOT encode the
   y-coordinate of any of the eight points of small order, whatever the
   sign bit (bad-key).  With a key of small order, anyone can produce a
   signature that verifies; a y-coordinate of p or more gives one point
   two encodings.  [RFC8032] requires the first check (Section 5.1.3 of
   [RFC8032]) but not the second, and libraries differ on both, so a
   verifier performs both itself.

   A verifier is told which principals it trusts by the thumbprints of
   their passkeys, and which recorders it trusts by the digests of their
   key sets.  The encoding defines both (Section 11.3).

4.5.  Hybrid Signatures

   A receipt, a countersignature, a delegation, and an acknowledgement
   carry two signatures over the same content, in this order: first
   Ed25519 (Section 5.1 of [RFC8032]), verified as Section 4.6 requires;
   then ML-DSA-87, the pure (not pre-hashed) ML-DSA.Verify of [FIPS204]
   with an empty context, as Section 5 of [RFC9964] requires.  A signed
   tree head carries a third: SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s, the pure slh_verify of
   [FIPS205] with an empty context.  An empty context is the zero-length
   string, not a single zero byte.  Each signature is over its own
   signing input, which the encoding defines and which covers the kind
   of record (Section 11.1).  Each is verified with the key at the same
   position in the applicable key set: the agent's or a service's keys
   in the permission, a helper's keys in its delegation, or the
   recorder's keys in the head.  Every signature MUST verify; a
   signature that fails is a problem (signature-invalid).









Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 10]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


   A signature is exactly 64 bytes (Ed25519), 4,627 bytes (ML-DSA-87),
   or 29,792 bytes (SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s).  An Ed25519, ML-DSA-87, or SLH-
   DSA-SHA2-256s signature of any other length is a problem (signature-
   invalid) on every verifier, whether or not the verifier implements
   the method.  This rule covers every signature of a record, a
   passkey's Ed25519 signature included; it does not reach a TSA's
   signature inside a time-stamp (Section 9.3).

   A signature of the right length whose algorithm the verifier lacks is
   not verified (Section 10.1).  An entry none of whose signatures was
   verified is not evidence: what it says MUST NOT be shown as fact.

4.6.  Ed25519 Verification

   [RFC8032] leaves some verification choices to implementations, and
   implementations that choose differently accept different sets of
   signatures.  With public key A, signature halves R and S, message M,
   base point B, group order L, and n*P for the point P multiplied by
   the integer n, a verifier MUST:

   1.  refuse S unless, read as a little-endian integer, it is less than
       L;
   2.  refuse A or R unless it decodes to a point on the curve, which
       includes refusing an encoded y of p or more (Section 5.1.3 of
       [RFC8032]);
   3.  refuse A or R of small order, that is, one of the eight points
       whose order divides 8, including the identity element; and
   4.  accept only if S*B = R + k*A, where k is the SHA-512 hash of the
       32-byte encoding of R, then the 32-byte encoding of A, then M,
       read as a little-endian integer and reduced modulo L before the
       multiplication.  Comparing the encoding of S*B - k*A with the 32
       bytes of R is the same test, since rule 2 makes R's encoding
       unique.  A signature that satisfies only the equation multiplied
       by the cofactor 8, 8*S*B = 8*R + 8*k*A, is refused.

   Rules 1 and 2 restate Section 5.1.7 of [RFC8032]; rules 3 and 4
   choose where it leaves choices.  These rules apply to every Ed25519
   signature, a passkey's included; a key whose point is the sum of a
   point of large order and a point of small order (a mixed-order key)
   is not refused by these rules.

4.7.  Passkey Signatures

   A permission, an approval, and a cancellation carry one signature by
   the principal's passkey.  To sign, the signer requests an assertion
   from the passkey with user verification "required".  The challenge is
   the SHA-256 hash of the record's signing input, which the encoding
   defines and which covers the record's kind and content.  The record
   carries the authenticator data, the client data, and the signature
   exactly as the passkey returned them (Section 11.1).

   A verifier checks the signature against the "key", "rpId", and
   "origin" of the "issuer" of the permission concerned.  It follows
   Section 7.2 of [WebAuthn] as far as it applies to a later check with
   no state, and MUST confirm that:

   1.  the client data is UTF-8 with no byte order mark and is a JSON
       object, read by a JSON parser: white space and member order are
       not constrained, and a repeated member is read as its last
       occurrence (passkey-bad-data);
   2.  its "type" is "webauthn.get" (passkey-wrong-type);
   3.  its "challenge" is the base64url of the SHA-256 hash of the
       record's signing input (passkey-challenge-mismatch);
   4.  its "origin" is the same string as the issuer's, and
       "crossOrigin" is not the JSON value true (passkey-origin-
       mismatch); other members are not used;
   5.  the authenticator data is at least 37 bytes (passkey-bad-data)
       and begins with the SHA-256 hash of "rpId" (passkey-rpid-
       mismatch);
   6.  its flags have "user present" (bit 0) and "user verified" (bit 2)
       set (passkey-user-not-present, passkey-user-not-verified) and
       "attested credential data" (bit 6) clear.  Without "extension
       data" (bit 7), the authenticator data is exactly 37 bytes
       (passkey-bad-data).  Other flags and any further bytes are not
       examined; in particular, the [WebAuthn] rule that "backup state"
       (bit 4) is clear whenever "backup eligible" (bit 3) is clear is
       not checked; and
   7.  the signature verifies over the authenticator data followed by
       the SHA-256 hash of the client data (signature-invalid).  An
       ES256 signature is an Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm
       (ECDSA) signature (Section 3.4 of [RFC7518]) stored as an ECDSA-
       Sig-Value in strict Distinguished Encoding Rules (DER) [X690]:
       one SEQUENCE with a one-byte length, holding two positive
       INTEGERs of 1 to 33 bytes in their shortest form, with nothing
       after them (passkey-bad-data).  Either value of s that verifies
       is accepted.  An RS256 signature is as Section 3.3 of [RFC7518].






Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 12]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


   The signature counter, the credential identifier, and the user handle
   are not used.  For an approval or a cancellation, a failure is
   reported as approval-invalid or cancellation-invalid.  If the
   verifier was given thumbprints of trusted passkeys, the issuer's key
   MUST be one (issuer-not-expected); if it was given none, it MUST say
   that the key was not compared with a trusted key.  The "rpId" and
   "origin" of the issuer are part of what a verifier trusts, not only
   the thumbprint (Section 18).

4.8.  Records Relied On

   An entry that relies on another record names it by its digest.  The
   named record MUST be earlier in the log and sound; otherwise the
   entry has a problem:

   *  slip-missing or slip-unusable, for a permission that is missing or
      not sound;
   *  pass-missing, for a delegation that is missing or not sound, or
      pass-mismatch for a delegation under another permission; and
   *  cancellation-not-found, for a cancellation.

   A permission or a delegation is judged as it stood when it was itself
   read.  A problem that a later entry adds to it (dated-after-stamp,
   Section 9.3) changes nothing for the entries that rely on it, though
   it remains a problem of the log.  A cancellation named by an
   acknowledgement is judged again once the whole log has been read; if
   it has a problem then, the acknowledgement is reported cancellation-
   not-found.

5.  The Permission

   A permission's content holds exactly these members, all of them
   required except "passes":

   "type":  "provared.slip.v0"
   "id":  An identifier.
   "issuer":  The principal: exactly "key", "name", "origin", and
      "rpId", as specified below.
   "agent":  Exactly "keys" (a key set) and "name", and optionally
      "software": 1 to 16 maps {"name", "sha256"}, digests of the
      program and settings the principal approved.  It is a statement,
      not proof of what ran.
   "actions":  1 to 64 action names, none repeated.
   "limits":  0 to 64 limits (Section 5.1).
   "requires":  0 to 64 conditions (Section 5.1).
   "never":  0 to 16 names, none repeated (Section 5.1).
   "with":  0 to 64 services, {"id", "name"} or {"id", "keys", "name"};




Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 13]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


      "id" has the form of an action name, may begin "provared.", and is
      unique.  A service with no "keys" cannot countersign.
   "passes":  An integer from 1 to 10 (Section 7).
   "validFrom", "validUntil":  Times; "validUntil" is later than
      "validFrom".  The permission covers "validFrom" up to, but not
      including, "validUntil".
   "purpose":  Text of 1 to 1,000 code points.

   "rpId" is the WebAuthn Relying Party Identifier (RP ID): 1 to 253
   lower-case ASCII letters, digits, ".", and "-", beginning and ending
   with a letter or a digit.

   The member "origin" is at most 300 characters and MUST have the
   following form (bad-field).  It is the form in which a browser
   serializes the origin of a site named by a domain name (Section 6.2
   of [RFC6454]), and a verifier can check it without a parser of web
   addresses and without Unicode tables:

   *  the scheme, which MUST be "https", or "http" only with the host
      "localhost";
   *  "://";
   *  the host: 1 to 253 characters, made of labels separated by ".",
      each label 1 to 63 lower-case ASCII letters, digits, and "-",
      neither beginning nor ending with "-", and the last label not all
      digits; a name in another script appears in its "xn--" form and is
      taken as it is; and
   *  optionally ":" followed by a port, only where the port is not the
      scheme's default (443 for "https", 80 for "http"), in digits with
      no leading zero, from 1 to 65,535.

   Nothing else may follow.  The host MUST be "rpId" or end with "."
   followed by "rpId".

   An action name is 1 to 64 lower-case ASCII letters, digits, ".", "-",
   and "_", beginning with a letter or a digit.  Names are compared
   exactly.  Names beginning "provared." are reserved for the shared
   list of Appendix B, and an action name that begins so MUST be on it
   (bad-field).  Any other member is a problem for a core verifier (bad-
   field); this includes the members by which [FORMAT] covers fields
   (Section 15).

5.1.  Limits, Conditions, and Prohibitions

   A limit names an "action" from "actions" (bad-field) and holds
   exactly one of "max", "each", and "count":






Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 14]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


  {"action","max","unit"}        the sum of the amounts is at most "max"
  {"action","each","unit"}       no single amount exceeds "each"
  {"action","count"}             at most "count" receipts ("count" >= 1)
  {"action","max","per","unit"}  as "max", within any period of "per"
                                 seconds
  {"action","count","per"}       as "count", within any period of "per"
                                 seconds

   "per" is from 1 to 31,622,400 seconds (366 days).  Two limits MUST
   NOT agree in all three of: "action"; which of "max", "each", or
   "count" they hold; and "per", or its absence (bad-field).  Every
   "unit" named for one action, in limits and conditions, MUST be the
   same: a receipt gives one amount.

   A condition is {"need": "countersignature"} or {"need": "approval"}.
   It may hold "action", which MUST be one of "actions" (bad-field);
   without it, the condition applies to every action.  The members
   "above" and "unit" are either both present or both absent, and are
   present only together with "action"; they restrict the condition to
   amounts greater than "above" in that unit.  Two conditions MUST NOT
   have the same "need" together with the same "action", or the same
   "need" and both without "action" (bad-field).  The need
   "countersignature" asks for the service's countersignature
   (Section 6); "approval" asks for the principal's approval.

   "never" names kinds of action and rules of conduct from Appendix B.
   A permission MUST NOT allow, in "actions", a shared action of a kind
   that its "never" names (bad-field).  A rule of conduct is the
   principal's signed instruction; no check of a record shows that it
   was kept.

6.  Action Receipts

   A receipt's content holds exactly:

   "type":  "provared.stub.v0"
   "id":  An identifier.
   "slip":  The digest of the permission.
   "seq":  Its place in its chain, from 0.
   "previous":  The digest of the receipt before it; present if and only
      if "seq" is not 0.
   "action":  An action name.
   "amount":  Optional: an amount.
   "with":  Optional: the "id" of a service.
   "details":  Optional: 1 to 32 maps {"name", "sha256"}, the documents
      involved.
   "approval":  Optional: the digest of an approval.
   "pass":  Optional: the digest of the delegation a helper acts under.



Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 15]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


   "terms":  Optional, with "with": the digest of a service's terms
      [FORMAT].
   "when":  The time of the action, as the agent states it.

   Document names that begin "provared." are reserved.  In a receipt's
   or an approval's "details", a name that begins so MUST be one of the
   seven names of Section 27 of [FORMAT]: "provared.arguments",
   "provared.http-request", "provared.http-response", "provared.trace-
   id", "provared.request-id", "provared.event-id", or "provared.error"
   (bad-field).  Of these, only "provared.trace-id", "provared.request-
   id", and "provared.event-id" may be named more than once in one
   record (bad-field).  [FORMAT] states the exact text whose digest each
   reserved name carries; a core verifier checks the names, not the
   digests.

   The permission and any delegation named are records relied on
   (Section 4.8).  Both signatures MUST verify with the agent's keys or,
   for a helper, with the "to" keys of its delegation (signature-
   invalid).  A core verifier accepts no terms entry, so it reports a
   receipt that holds "terms" as terms-not-found.

   The receipts of the permission's own agent form one chain, and those
   under each delegation another.  In each chain, in log order, the
   first has "seq" 0.  Each later one has the next "seq" and names the
   one before in "previous" (chain-broken), and is not dated before it
   (time-went-backwards).  A receipt with a problem keeps its place in
   its chain, provided its content could be read and the records it
   relies on were found: the next receipt is checked against it.  So one
   break is reported once.

   A countersignature has exactly the content {"stub": the receipt's
   digest, "type": "provared.countersignature.v0", "when": a time}, and
   states that the action took place with the service.  It is carried in
   the receipt's log entry and signed with the key set the permission
   gives for the service the receipt names in "with".  A verifier MUST
   confirm "stub" (countersignature-wrong-stub), that such keys exist
   (countersignature-not-possible), and both signatures
   (countersignature-invalid).  A receipt without a countersignature is
   one-sided.  This is a reported state, not a problem: such a receipt
   shows only what the agent stated.











Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 16]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


   Where an executor (Section 2) is to countersign, a receipt names the
   executor in "with", since a countersignature is verified with the
   keys that the permission gives for the service the receipt names.
   The party beyond the executor, if there is one, is then not named in
   "with", and the permission's "with" does not limit which such parties
   the agent deals with; a reserved document name such as
   "provared.http-request" can bind the receipt to the call the executor
   made.  Where that party countersigns instead, the receipt names it,
   and the executor is not named.

   An approval is the principal's approval of exactly one action.  Its
   content holds exactly "type" ("provared.approval.v0"), "id", "slip",
   "action", and "when", and optionally "amount", "with", and "details".
   It is signed with the permission's passkey and carried in the
   receipt's entry, and the receipt names it by its digest.  The receipt
   MUST name the approval in its entry (approval-mismatch), and an
   approval it names MUST be there (approval-not-found).  The approval's
   "slip", "action", "amount", "with", and "details" MUST match the
   receipt's, each present in both or neither (approval-mismatch).  Its
   "id" MUST NOT be that of an approval in an earlier entry (approval-
   reused) or of another record (duplicate-id).  Its passkey signature
   MUST verify against the permission's "issuer" (approval-invalid).  It
   MUST NOT be dated more than 300 seconds after the receipt, which was
   signed after it (approval-dated-after-stub).  An approval shows that
   the passkey approved the action, not that the principal read it.

7.  Delegation

   "passes" says how many times in a row a permission may be delegated:
   1 lets its agent delegate to a helper, 2 lets that helper delegate
   once more, up to 10.  Without it, a permission may not be delegated.
   A delegation's content holds exactly:

   "type":  "provared.pass.v0"
   "id":  An identifier.
   "slip":  The permission's digest.
   "from":  The digest of the delegation its writer acts under; absent
      when the writer is the permission's own agent.
   "to":  The helper: exactly {"keys": key set, "name": name}.
   "actions":  As in a permission.
   "limits":  As in a permission, naming only these "actions".
   "validFrom", "validUntil":  Its validity, as in a permission.
   "when":  When it was written.








Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 17]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


   It is signed by its writer: with the agent's keys or, with "from",
   the "to" keys of that delegation (signature-invalid).  The permission
   and the delegation in "from" are records relied on (Section 4.8).
   Its depth is 1 without "from", otherwise one more than that of
   "from"; a depth above 10 is a problem (pass-too-deep).

   A verifier reports these findings for a delegation:

   *  pass-not-allowed, if its depth exceeds "passes" (taken as 0 when
      "passes" is absent);
   *  pass-wider, if any of its "actions" is not among its writer's, or
      its validity begins earlier or ends later than its writer's;
   *  outside-valid-time, if "when" is outside its writer's validity;
      and
   *  after-cancellation, as Section 8 sets out.

   "Its writer's" means the permission's, or, with "from", the
   delegation's named there.

   A receipt under a delegation is compared with the permission exactly
   as the agent's own receipts are, and shares their totals, conditions,
   and "never".  It is also compared with its own delegation and with
   every delegation above it, each treated as a permission for its
   "actions", "limits", and validity; the totals of a delegation include
   everything done under delegations from it.  A finding code already
   reported for a receipt is not repeated.  Every receipt under a
   delegation reported pass-not-allowed, or under a delegation below it,
   is reported pass-not-allowed.  Because the limits of the permission
   and of every delegation above always apply, a delegation never widens
   what the verifier accepts as within the permission.

8.  Cancellation and Acknowledgement

   A cancellation has exactly the content {"id", "slip", "type":
   "provared.cancellation.v0", "when"}, and is signed with the
   permission's passkey and verified against its "issuer" (cancellation-
   invalid); the permission is a record relied on (Section 4.8).  Its
   entry may hold "stamps" over its digest (Section 9.3), which the
   principal can obtain at once.

   The effective cancellation of a permission is the first cancellation
   of it in the log that is sound when read and whose passkey signature,
   and that of its permission, were verified.  Every receipt and
   delegation under the permission that follows the effective
   cancellation in the log is reported after-cancellation.






Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 18]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


   Section 22 of [FORMAT] adds a stricter rule for a time-stamped
   cancellation.  A receipt before it in the log is also reported,
   unless a time-stamp on an earlier head shows that the receipt existed
   by the cancellation's time, within 300 seconds.  This document does
   not specify that rule (Section 16).  Without that rule, a core
   verifier can miss receipts made after a cancellation.

   A cancellation does not show that the agent was told; an
   acknowledgement is the agent's signed statement that it was.  Its
   content holds exactly "cancellation" (a digest), "id", "slip", "type"
   ("provared.acknowledgement.v0"), and "when", and optionally "pass".
   It is signed with the agent's keys or a helper's (signature-invalid).
   The permission, any delegation named, and the cancellation, which
   MUST cancel the same permission (cancellation-not-found), are records
   relied on (Section 4.8).  An acknowledgement changes no finding, and
   its "when" is not compared with the cancellation's; its signer SHOULD
   NOT date it before the last receipt or delegation its agent wrote.
   Section 26 of [FORMAT] sets out how a verifier uses it with the
   principal's own copy.

9.  Logs, Signed Tree Heads, and Time-Stamps

9.1.  The Log

   A log is a sequence of entries, numbered from 0.  Each entry holds
   one record, with what is carried beside it: a permission; an action
   receipt, optionally with a countersignature and an approval; a
   delegation; a cancellation, optionally with time-stamps; an
   acknowledgement; or a signed tree head, optionally with time-stamps.
   Any other entry is a problem (unknown-entry).  A core verifier
   reports the four further kinds of [FORMAT] ("refusal", "terms",
   "vouching", "withdrawal") in the same way, and so never calls such a
   log intact.  The same permission MUST NOT appear twice (duplicate-
   slip), nor the same delegation (duplicate-id).  A log MUST NOT exceed
   100,000 entries (too-large).  Section 11.4 gives the form of a log in
   the JSON encoding.

   The leaves of the tree are the entries as the encoding writes them,
   in log order; the root is the Merkle Tree Hash of Section 2.1.1 of
   [RFC9162] with SHA-256.  Inclusion and consistency proofs are checked
   against a size as well as a root, so the two are kept together.  A
   verifier given a root or a size to expect MUST compare it (root-
   mismatch).








Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 19]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


9.2.  Signed Tree Heads

   A signed tree head's content holds exactly "type"
   ("provared.seal.v0"), "id", "by" (exactly {"keys": the recorder's key
   set, "name"}), "size" (at least 1), "root", "previous" (the previous
   head's digest, absent for the first), and "when".  A head is signed
   with the three keys in "by" and appended as the next entry, so each
   head covers those before it.  A verifier holding the whole log MUST
   confirm that:

   1.  the three signatures verify (signature-invalid);
   2.  "size" is its own place in the log, and "root" the root of the
       entries before it (seal-mismatch);
   3.  "previous" names the nearest earlier head, or is absent if there
       is none (seal-chain-broken);
   4.  "when" is not before that head's (time-went-backwards); and
   5.  if given digests of trusted recorders' key sets, that of
       "by.keys" is one (sealer-not-expected); if given none, it MUST
       say that the keys were not compared with trusted keys.

   Section 21 of [FORMAT] adds rules that compare a head's "when" with
   the times its entries state and with earlier heads' time-stamps
   (time-went-backwards).  This document does not specify them
   (Section 16).

9.3.  Time-Stamps

   "stamps" holds 1 to 4 time-stamps, none repeated.  A time-stamp is
   either an [RFC3161] TimeStampToken or a block time-stamp of [FORMAT].
   A TimeStampToken is at most 12,288 bytes; its "messageImprint" has
   the hashAlgorithm SHA-256 and a hashedMessage equal to the 32 bytes
   of the record's digest, not hashed again.  A block time-stamp holds
   exactly "block" and "proof", both bytes (Section 11.4); a core
   verifier does not count it, and so answers no to the second question
   of Section 10.1.  Time-stamps are read only if none of the record's
   own signatures failed.  A verifier MUST confirm that:

   1.  the token is one element with nothing after it, laid out as
       [RFC3161] and [RFC5652] require: signed data of version 3 with
       exactly one signer, of version 1 or 3, enclosing a TSTInfo of
       version 1 (stamp-bad-data);
   2.  it meets these rules of DER [X690] (stamp-bad-data):
       *  definite lengths in their shortest form, with a length field
          of at most four bytes, and single-byte tags;

       *  every constructed element exactly filled by its parts;





Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 20]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


       *  every INTEGER and OBJECT IDENTIFIER in its shortest form;
          every BOOLEAN 0x00 or 0xFF; every NULL empty; every BIT STRING
          with at most 7 unused bits;

       *  genTime and each certificate's validity times in the one form
          DER gives them, denoting a moment that exists;

       *  the public key of the signing certificate written exactly as
          its own specification sets out, with no unused bits: RSA as
          Section 2.3.1 of [RFC3279], with the parameters present and
          NULL and two positive INTEGERs with nothing after them; ECDSA
          as Section 2.2 of [RFC5480], as an uncompressed point; Ed25519
          as Section 4 of [RFC8410], with no parameters and 32 bytes;
          and

       *  at most 4,000 elements, none more than 24 levels deep, in each
          of the signed data, the enclosed TSTInfo (read from its OCTET
          STRING), and an RSA public key; at most 8 digest algorithms, 8
          certificates, 16 signed attributes, 4 values in one attribute,
          8 identifiers in a signing-certificate attribute, and 16
          TSTInfo extensions.
       The order of SET OF elements and the omission of DEFAULT values
       are not checked;
   3.  the "messageImprint" is as above (stamp-wrong-data);
   4.  the signed attributes hold a content type of id-ct-TSTInfo, and
       the TSTInfo's message digest made with the signer's digest
       algorithm (SHA-256, SHA-384, or SHA-512).  They also hold
       ESSCertIDv2 ([RFC5035], [RFC5816]), with SHA-256 (the default),
       SHA-384, or SHA-512, or ESSCertID ([RFC2634]), with SHA-1,
       ESSCertIDv2 being used if both are present; the first identifier
       in the attribute names the certificate, which the token carries;
       the issuerSerial is not compared.  Each of these attributes
       appears at most once and holds exactly one value, as [RFC5652]
       and [RFC5035] require (stamp-invalid);
   5.  the signature over the signed attributes verifies with that
       certificate's key (stamp-invalid).  The signature algorithm names
       the signer's digest algorithm (for RSA, rsaEncryption is also
       accepted).  The accepted methods are RSASSA-PKCS1-v1_5 with a
       modulus of 2,048 to 8,192 bits and an odd public exponent from 3
       to 2^32 - 1 (with an exponent of 1, anyone can produce a
       signature that verifies); ECDSA on P-256 or P-384; and Ed25519
       [RFC8419].  An Ed25519 key that Section 4.4 refuses, or a key of
       any other kind or on any other curve, is refused (stamp-invalid);
       and
   6.  "genTime" lies within the certificate's validity, the seconds
       field of any "accuracy" is at most 300, and no TSTInfo extension
       is critical (stamp-invalid).  The certificate's extensions are
       not examined, since the certificate is trusted by its digest.



Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 21]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


   A verifier is told which TSAs it trusts by the digest of each TSA's
   certificate in DER; it builds no chain, checks no revocation, and
   asks no outside party.  A time-stamp counts only if its TSA is
   trusted and every signature of its record was verified (for a
   cancellation, also its permission's).  A sound time-stamp from a TSA
   not trusted MUST be shown as such, with its certificate's digest; a
   time-stamp that does not count MUST NOT be used as a time.  Times are
   taken to the second, rounded down.

   A counted time-stamp at time T shows that the record existed by T.
   On a sound signed tree head, it also shows that every entry before
   the head existed by T.  An entry existed by the earliest such T of
   any later sound head.  A verifier MUST report "existed by", never
   "existed at", and MUST say which entries the last counted time-stamp
   reaches.  It MUST report dated-after-stamp for a head or a
   cancellation whose "when" is more than 300 seconds after the earliest
   time its counted time-stamps state, and for an entry whose "when", or
   the "when" of a countersignature or approval carried in it, is more
   than 300 seconds after the time by which the entry existed.

10.  Verification

10.1.  The Three Answers

   A verifier is given the log and, optionally, the size and root it
   expects, the thumbprints of the passkeys it trusts, and the digests
   of the key sets of the recorders and of the certificates of the TSAs
   it trusts.  It makes no network request.  It gives three answers,
   kept apart:

   1.  Was a problem found?  Yes if any entry, or the log, has one.
   2.  Was everything verified?  Yes only if every entry was read as far
       as its signatures, and no signature or time-stamp in the log was
       left unverified because the verifier does not implement its
       method.
   3.  Did each action stay within its permission?  Yes only if no
       problem was found, every receipt and delegation was compared, and
       none gave a finding.

   A verifier MUST call a log intact only if no problem was found and
   everything was verified.  A receipt or delegation is compared only if
   it is sound, at least one of its signatures was verified and none
   failed, and its permission's passkey signature was verified.  So the
   third answer can be yes while the second is no: for example, a
   verifier without ML-DSA-87 compares receipts on their Ed25519
   signatures alone.  When the second answer is no, a verifier MUST say
   that the third rests only on the signatures it verified, and MUST
   name the signing methods it lacked.



Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 22]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


   A verifier checks each record's encoding (in the JSON encoding, its
   envelope, labels, and content, Section 11), then its members, and
   then finds the records it relies on, in that order.  At the first
   problem in those steps, it reports that problem's code and checks
   nothing further in that entry.  Once an entry's members have been
   read, and before the records it relies on are looked up, its "id" is
   compared with the identifiers seen so far and then recorded; an
   approval's "id" is compared and recorded once the approval has been
   matched to its receipt.  Every other problem of an entry (a
   signature, its place in its chain, a countersignature, an approval, a
   repeated "id") is reported.  Anything the verifier did not expect is
   a problem (check-failed), never a pass.  Each problem and finding is
   reported with its entry's number.  Two verifiers agree if they give
   the same three answers, find problems at the same entries, and report
   the same findings for each entry.

10.2.  Findings

   A verifier reports these findings at the receipt concerned:

   action-not-allowed:  "action" is not in "actions".
   prohibited:         "action" is a shared action of a kind that
                       "never" names.
   party-not-allowed:  "with" names no service of the permission.
   outside-valid-time:  "when" is outside the permission's validity.
   amount-missing:     A limit with a "unit", or a condition with
                       "above", applies to the action, and the receipt
                       has no amount in that unit (once a receipt).
   over-limit, over-each-limit, over-count-limit:  The running total
                       exceeds "max"; the amount exceeds "each"; with
                       this receipt, the number exceeds "count".
   over-period-limit:  Within the period ending at this receipt, the
                       total exceeds "max" or the number "count".
   countersignature-missing, approval-missing:  A condition applies, and
                       the entry holds no countersignature, or no
                       approval.
   after-cancellation, pass-not-allowed:  As Section 8 and Section 7 set
                       out.

   A condition with "above" applies only if the amount is in its unit
   and greater than "above"; an amount missing or in another unit is
   amount-missing.  Only compared receipts count toward totals.

   Totals and counts without "per" accumulate in log order over every
   compared receipt for the action under the permission, across all
   chains including the current one.  Once a total is exceeded, every
   later receipt for that action is reported too.  A limit with "per" P
   is evaluated for each compared receipt X, dated t, over the period



Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 23]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


   that ends at X.  That period holds: every receipt for the action, in
   any chain and anywhere in the log, dated later than t - P and earlier
   than t; those dated exactly t that precede X in the log; and X
   itself.  Because a later entry may state an earlier time, period
   limits are settled only once every receipt has been read.  Periods
   are counted in seconds; there are no calendar days and no time zones.

11.  The JSON Encoding

   This section specifies the JSON encoding: how records, keys, and logs
   are written as JSON [RFC8259], and how signatures are carried, with
   JWS [RFC7515].

11.1.  Envelope and Labels

   Every record is a JWS in the General JWS JSON Serialization
   (Section 7.2.1 of [RFC7515]).  It MUST hold exactly the members
   "payload" and "signatures".  Each element of "signatures" MUST hold
   exactly "protected" and "signature" and, for a passkey signature,
   also "header", which holds exactly "authenticatorData" and
   "clientDataJSON" (bad-envelope).  A passkey's authenticator data and
   client data are stored there, and its signature in "signature", each
   exactly as returned.  The signing input of every signature is the JWS
   Signing Input (Section 5.1 of [RFC7515]) formed from "protected" and
   "payload" as carried.

   Every value written in base64url, in a record's envelope or content
   or in a log entry, is that of Section 5 of [RFC4648], without
   padding, and is read strictly: a verifier MUST refuse padding, a
   character outside the alphabet, a length that leaves 1 when divided
   by 4, and unused bits that are not zero (bad-base64url; in a key,
   bad-key; in "authenticatorData" and "clientDataJSON", passkey-bad-
   data).  The decoded "signature" of an Ed25519, ML-DSA-87, or SLH-DSA-
   SHA2-256s signature MUST have its method's length (Section 4.5;
   signature-invalid).

   The protected header of each signature, once decoded, MUST be exactly
   the UTF-8 bytes of {"typ":"<typ>","alg":"<alg>"}, with no white space
   and the two members in that order, where <typ> and <alg> are as
   follows.  This header is a fixed text, compared as bytes; it is
   deliberately not in the canonical member order of Section 11.2.  A
   verifier compares bytes and interprets nothing else.  A header that
   is not a known text is a problem (unknown-type).  One that belongs to
   another kind of record than the one expected is a problem too
   (payload-type-mismatch).  The number and order of the signatures are
   those of Section 4.1 (bad-signatures-layout).





Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 24]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


   <typ> is "vnd.provared.X.v0+json", with X as in Section 4.1: the
   subtype of the record's media type, written without its
   "application/" prefix as Section 4.1.9 of [RFC7515] allows.  The
   placeholder <alg> names the method of the signature: "Ed25519", "ML-
   DSA-87", "SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s", or "prova.red/webauthn/v0" for a
   passkey.  A passkey's signature is its assertion signature over the
   authenticator data and the client data (Section 4.7); the JWS Signing
   Input enters it only through the challenge.  The "typ" value is used
   for explicit typing (Section 3.11 of [RFC8725]); since it is signed,
   every signature covers the kind of record.  The name "Ed25519" is
   registered by [RFC9864] and "ML-DSA-87" by [RFC9964].  The name
   "prova.red/webauthn/v0" is collision-resistant (Section 4.1.1 of
   [RFC7515]) and is not registered.  The name "SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s",
   which [FIPS205] gives the parameter set, is neither registered nor
   collision-resistant, one of which Section 4.1.1 of [RFC7515] says an
   "alg" value should be; this document fixes it as a private-use value,
   compared byte for byte.  It follows the naming of
   [I-D.ietf-cose-sphincs-plus], which registers the 128s parameter sets
   with the same key form (AKP), pure mode, and the empty context, so
   that a registration of this name with that meaning would be
   compatible.

11.2.  Content and Values

   The content of a record is the decoded "payload", and its digest is
   the SHA-256 hash of those bytes.  It MUST be a JSON object in UTF-8,
   in the canonical form of [RFC8785]: a verifier serializes it again
   and refuses it if a single byte differs (payload-not-canonical).
   This refuses repeated member names, white space, and members out of
   order.  The content MUST hold only objects, arrays, strings, and
   integers from 0 to 2^53 - 1.  It holds no "true", "false", "null",
   fractions, exponents, or negative numbers, and no unpaired surrogate,
   even escaped.  Objects and arrays MUST NOT nest more than 8 levels
   deep, the content itself being the first (payload-not-canonical).
   The signed bytes are verified as signed; the canonical form is a
   test, never a rewrite.

   A map is written as an object, a list as an array, text as a string,
   and an integer as a number.  The values of Section 4.3 are written
   so:

   Bytes:  In base64url, read strictly as Section 11.1 sets out.
   Time:  "YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ", an [RFC3339] date-time (bad-field).
   Key:  A JSON Web Key (JWK) [RFC7517] of exactly one of the shapes
      below, with no other member (bad-key).






Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 25]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


   {"alg":"Ed25519","crv":"Ed25519","kty":"OKP","x":X}     X: 32 bytes
   {"alg":"ML-DSA-87","kty":"AKP","pub":P}               P: 2592 bytes
   {"alg":"SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s","kty":"AKP","pub":P}         P: 64 bytes
   {"alg":"ES256","crv":"P-256","kty":"EC","x":X,"y":Y}  X, Y: 32 bytes
   {"alg":"RS256","e":"AQAB","kty":"RSA","n":N}     N: 2048-8192 bits

   These follow [RFC8037] (the Octet Key Pair, "OKP", key type),
   Section 3 of [RFC9964] (the Algorithm Key Pair, "AKP", key type), and
   Section 6 of [RFC7518]; "n" has no leading zero byte.  A key set is
   an array of keys.

   A record MUST NOT exceed 65,536 characters, counted as the length of
   "payload" plus the lengths of every string in its signature entries,
   "header" included (too-large).

11.3.  Thumbprints and Key-Set Digests

   A passkey is named by its key thumbprint: [RFC7638] with SHA-256,
   over the members required by Section 3.2 of [RFC7638], Section 2 of
   [RFC8037] for "OKP", and Section 6 of [RFC9964] for "AKP".  A
   recorder is named by the digest of its key set: the SHA-256 hash of
   the key set written in the canonical form of Section 11.2.

11.4.  Logs

   A log is UTF-8 text, one entry to a line, each line ended by a line
   feed except perhaps the last.  A log with no entry, an empty line, or
   a byte order mark is a problem (not-json); so is a line ending in a
   carriage return (line-not-canonical).  Each line is a JSON object in
   the canonical form of Section 11.2, with records as values (line-not-
   canonical).  It holds exactly the members of one kind of entry
   (Section 9.1): "slip" (a permission); "stub", optionally with
   "countersignature" and "approval" (an action receipt); "pass" (a
   delegation); "cancellation", optionally with "stamps";
   "acknowledgement"; or "seal" (a signed tree head), optionally with
   "stamps" (unknown-entry).  A line MUST NOT exceed 131,072 bytes (too-
   large).  The leaves of the tree are the lines' bytes, without line
   feeds.

   In "stamps", a TimeStampToken is a string, in base64url.  A block
   time-stamp is an object of exactly "block" and "proof", each a string
   (bad-field) in base64url (Section 11.1).

12.  Other Encodings

   Another encoding could carry the same content model: for example, one
   using COSE_Sign (Section 4.1 of [RFC9052]) over deterministically
   encoded CBOR (Section 4.2 of [RFC8949]).  This document does not
   define one.  Such an encoding has to define:

   1.  the bytes of a record's content, in one form that a verifier
       tests, so that a record's digest is fixed; it may write member
       names in another fixed form, such as short integer labels;
   2.  how the signatures are carried, in the number and order of
       Section 4.1, and the signing input of each, which covers the kind
       of record (with COSE, perhaps by the "typ" header parameter
       [RFC9596]) and its content;
   3.  how each signature names its method, in fixed bytes that a
       verifier compares: with COSE, the "alg" of each signer, which has
       values for Ed25519 [RFC9864] and ML-DSA-87 [RFC9964], but none
       yet for SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s or the passkey signature;
   4.  the passkey's challenge, the SHA-256 hash of that signing input,
       and where the authenticator data and client data are stored;
   5.  how bytes, times, and keys are written (with COSE, keys as
       COSE_Key), and the thumbprints ([RFC9679]) and key-set digests by
       which a verifier is told whom it trusts;
   6.  the form of a log and of its entries, which are the leaves of the
       tree; and
   7.  its size limits, and the codes for problems of its own form.

   The rest of the content model, the three answers, and the findings
   apply unchanged.  A record's digest depends on its encoding, so
   records that name one another, and the entries of one log, are in one
   encoding.  The thumbprints and key-set digests by which a verifier is
   told whom it trusts also depend on the encoding: a key's JWK
   thumbprint and its COSE_Key thumbprint differ.

14.  Design Rationale

   Passkeys are on the devices people use, resist phishing, and record
   user verification; no device offers a post-quantum passkey.  The JSON
   encoding is defined first because an implementation uses it, and JSON
   text suits a log of one entry to a line.  Two signatures side by side
   can each be verified by any library, and a verifier lacking one says
   so.  Composite signatures [I-D.ietf-jose-pq-composite-sigs] would
   make one of the two, but that draft pairs ML-DSA-87 only with ES384
   or Ed448, never with Ed25519.  Verification needs no network, because
   a record may be checked years later, when its writers have gone.
   Post-quantum signatures are used from the start: if Ed25519 is forged
   in future, a record carrying only Ed25519 could be made afterward and
   not told apart from a genuine one; SLH-DSA rests on hash functions



Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 30]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


   alone.  The tree is that of Certificate Transparency, the covering of
   fields in [FORMAT] that of [RFC9901], and the time-stamp that of the
   Time-Stamp Protocol.

15.  Parts Specified Elsewhere

   [FORMAT] specifies these parts, out of scope here: one-page proofs of
   single entries, with a permission's names and purpose covered by the
   disclosures of [RFC9901]; vouching records, by which an organization
   states that a key belongs to a name, and their withdrawal; a
   service's signed refusals and terms; time-stamps from a public
   blockchain; further date rules; the principal's own copy of a
   cancellation, handed to a verifier; the exact texts whose digests the
   reserved document names of Section 6 carry (Section 27 of [FORMAT]),
   which bind a receipt to a web request, an answer, a call's arguments,
   an identifier for the call from either side, or an error text; and
   the check before acting (Section 20 of [FORMAT]), by which the
   software around an agent asks, before an action, whether it would
   stay within the permission.  How records are carried, how keys are
   distributed, and what to do about a finding are out of scope too.

16.  Open Issues

   This section is to be removed before publishing as an RFC.

   1.  Labels: standards-tree media types [RFC6838], a registered
       passkey method, and the brand in the identifiers.  The brand
       stands in the media types, in the passkey method name, in the
       "type" values "provared.X.v0" of the content model (Section 4.1),
       and in the reserved action prefix "provared." of Section 5 and
       Appendix B.  On adoption, all of these would be renamed, with a
       "type" such as "agent-permission.v0" and a prefix chosen by the
       group; the "v0" label is what keeps the two from being confused,
       and the vendor-tree media types would then need no registration.
       [I-D.ietf-cose-sphincs-plus] registers SLH-DSA-SHA2-128s and SLH-
       DSA-SHAKE-128s, not SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s.
   2.  Canonical form: [RFC8785] is Informational (Independent stream),
       not in the downref registry; this document could define the
       narrowed form, which is short.
   3.  Client data: refusing repeated members, or the limited
       verification algorithm of [WebAuthn]; public suffixes; extension
       bytes; backup flags.
   4.  [RFC8419] requires SHA-512 with Ed25519 when signed attributes
       are present; Section 9.3 also accepts SHA-256 and SHA-384, which
       version 0.6.0 of the implementation refuses (Section 17).
   5.  Timing: whether to bring in the date rules of Section 21 of
       [FORMAT] and Section 22 of [FORMAT], and whether 300 seconds is
       the right allowance.



Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 31]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


   6.  A COSE encoding (Section 12), and detached payloads; a member for
       a correlation identifier shared with other audit records
       (Section 13); SCITT registration; consistency proofs
       (Section 2.1.4 of [RFC9162]); composite signatures; a registry
       for shared action names; whether to answer "within its
       permission" when not everything was verified.
   7.  Retries: no rule links the attempts of one operation.  Each
       attempt for which a receipt is written counts toward totals and

   8.  What a countersignature attests: "the action took place with" the

       outcome is known.  [I-D.gilda-audit-agent-audit-record] and

       followed it apart.

18.  Security Considerations

   Making or widening a permission requires a signature by the
   principal's passkey; making or changing any other record requires its
   signer's keys.  Without the keys that signed a chain, a receipt
   cannot be removed from the middle of the chain, moved within it, or
   changed without a verifier reporting it (chain-broken, signature-
   invalid).  Whoever holds those keys can re-sign the chain from any
   receipt onward.  Anyone can remove the last receipts of a chain,
   unless a head held by someone other than the recorder covers them, or



Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 33]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


   the service has kept a countersignature of one of them.  A verifier
   that holds a copy of a signed tree head, or of its size and root,
   obtained from someone other than the recorder, detects any change,
   addition, or removal before that head (root-mismatch).  Trusting the
   recorder's keys protects against everyone except the recorder, who
   can sign a shortened or reordered history.  A counted time-stamp
   shows that the entries before a sound head existed by its time.  Once
   a permission is covered by a head whose keys the verifier trusts, two
   of the head's three signatures, which are post-quantum, cover the
   permission's entry.  The passkey signature on the permission itself
   is classical.  What the head adds is that the permission, as it
   stands in the log, cannot be replaced afterward by anyone without the
   recorder's keys, even if its passkey signature later becomes
   forgeable; it does not make the principal's signature post-quantum.

   What this document does not protect against:

   No trusted keys:  With no trusted keys named, "intact" means only
      that the log is consistent with itself.  Anyone can make such a
      log.
   Omission, one side's word:  An agent that writes no receipt leaves no
      trace in its own log, and no check of that log shows the gap.
      Only another party's records can show it: a countersignature kept
      by the service, or the records of an executor (Section 2) that
      refuses any action the permission does not allow, or any action
      without a verified approval, and records each action it allows
      (the strongest class of [I-D.schrock-ep-authorization-receipts]
      has such an executor, which checks that document's own
      authorizations).  An executor's records show the gap only if the
      action cannot take place without the executor, and if the executor
      keeps them where the agent's side cannot change them.  A
      permission whose "with" names no service but the executor, and
      that requires a countersignature for every action, makes each
      receipt in the log that lacks the executor's countersignature a
      finding (countersignature-missing, or party-not-allowed for a
      receipt that names another party).  A receipt that was never
      written is found only by comparing the executor's own records with
      the log, which this document does not specify.  A cancellation the
      principal signed can be left out of the log by whoever keeps it,
      after which every later receipt reads as within the permission;
      only the principal's own copy of the cancellation, handed to the
      verifier (Section 22 of [FORMAT], out of scope here), answers
      this.  A one-sided receipt, an acknowledgement, and the times
      stated in records, on which period limits depend, rest on the
      signer's own statement.
   Truncation and split views:  The last head, with everything after it,





Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 34]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


      can be removed unnoticed unless someone else holds that head.  A
      recorder can keep two logs that differ after some entry, with
      heads for each; no consistency proof is defined.  Handing heads to
      others lets them be compared.
   One log only:  approval-reused and duplicate-id hold within one log,
      and so do totals, counts, and periods.  Receipts for one
      permission written into two logs, or into a log that was started
      afresh, are not added together: each log can be within its limits
      while the two together are not.  A recorder that keeps one log for
      each permission avoids this; the format does not close it.
   Service keys:  The keys of a service in "with" are stated by the
      principal.  A countersignature shows that a holder of those keys
      signed; it does not show who that holder is.  A principal who
      lists keys they control can countersign their own agent's
      receipts.  A compromise of a service's keys lets anyone
      countersign in its name: a countersignature is only as strong as
      the service's keys.
   Agent keys held by the recorder:  Such a recorder can write and sign
      another history.  A time-stamp then shows only when;
      countersignatures still stand against it.
   Passkeys:  A passkey shows that a device signed after verifying a
      user, not who held it, nor that the principal read what was
      signed; a compromised device or page can show one thing and have
      another signed.  The challenge is opaque to the principal.  Any
      party that can choose the challenge at an origin under the
      issuer's "rpId" (the site's operator, any script at that origin,
      or any subdomain of the "rpId") can obtain a signature on a record
      of its choosing by presenting it as a sign-in: the client data of
      a sign-in and of a record are the same shape.  So a passkey
      trusted for records SHOULD be created for an "rpId" used for
      nothing but signing records, the signing page SHOULD show the
      record before asking, and a verifier MAY be told the "origin" it
      expects as well as the thumbprint.  The counter is not used, so a
      cloned passkey goes unseen.  An "http://localhost" origin is for
      tests only.
   Time-stamp keys:  A TSA is trusted by certificate digest, without
      revocation, and its time is checked against the certificate's
      validity, not the verifier's clock.  A compromised TSA key can
      stamp any time; a verifier that learns of it MUST stop trusting
      it.  Up to four TSAs lessen the dependence on one.
   Renewal:  TSA signatures are classical, and certificates age; so the
      times by which entries existed rest on classical signatures and
      have no post-quantum protection.  A later head covers earlier
      heads' entries, time-stamps included, so stamping it by a newer
      TSA extends the evidence, as evidence records [RFC4998] do.
   Stripping:  Signatures are fixed in number, order, and label, and
      their keys are given elsewhere (Section 4.5), so removing or
      replacing one is caught.



Izmaylov                   Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 35]

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


   Ed25519 alone:  Without ML-DSA-87, what is compared rests on Ed25519
      alone.  Such a verifier cannot tell a record made with the agent's
      Ed25519 key alone from a genuine one.  A verifier whose library
      applies other rules than Section 4.6 MUST add the missing checks.
   Malleable signatures:  An ECDSA signature can be altered into another
      valid one.  A record digest covers only content, so no digest
      changes; the entry does, and a later head shows it.

   A verifier MUST treat every byte of a record as untrusted, and answer
   a failed check with a code, not a crash.  It SHOULD replace control,
   invisible, and direction-changing characters before showing text from
   a record.  Member names are plain strings: "__proto__" is like any
   other.

19.  Privacy Considerations

   Following [RFC6973]: keys repeat in every record a party signs, so
   records in different logs can be linked.  Document digests are not
   salted, so whoever can guess a document can confirm that a receipt
   names it; a writer can add a random value to a document first.  A TSA
   learns the digests it stamps, when, and who asked.  An entry cannot
   be erased without breaking later heads, so records should hold no
   more personal data than needed; names may be pseudonyms, and [FORMAT]
   defines covered names and purpose.  The members "origin" and "rpId"
   name the site where the principal signed.  A verifier makes no
   network request.

20.  IANA Considerations

   This document has no IANA actions.  On adoption, a later version is
   expected to request standards-tree media types [RFC6838] for each
   kind of record.  It would also request a JWS algorithm name for the
   passkey signature, and one for the third signature of a signed tree
   head (Section 16).

Appendix B.  Shared List

   Names that begin "provared." are reserved for this list; [FORMAT]
   also gives each action's meaning.  Shared action names are written
   below without their "provared." prefix: "data.read" stands for
   "provared.data.read".  Kinds and rules of conduct are written as
   shown, with no prefix; a "never" list may name any of them.

   "reads":  Looks at data without changing it.  Actions: "data.read",
      "data.search", "message.read", "calendar.read", "code.read",
      "web.read".
   "changes":  Creates or changes data in a way that can be put back.
      Actions: "data.create", "data.change", "message.draft",
      "calendar.change", "access.remove", "code.change",
      "system.configure".
   "destroys":  Removes data or a resource in a way that may not be put

Internet-Draft          Agent Permission Receipts           October 2026


      back.  Actions: "data.delete".
   "sends":  Sends a message or data to a person, to the public, or to
      another system.  Actions: "data.export", "message.send",
      "post.publish", "agent.instruct".
   "commits":  Binds the principal to something: an order, a booking, an
      agreement, an account.  Actions: "booking.make", "booking.cancel",
      "order.place", "order.cancel", "terms.accept", "form.submit",
      "account.create".
   "grants":  Gives someone or something access or permission.  Actions:
      "access.grant", "key.create".
   "runs":  Runs a program or a command.  Actions: "code.run",
      "code.release".
   "enters":  Signs in to a system or an account.  Actions:
      "account.sign-in".
   "impersonate":  Never claim to be a human being, or to be anyone
      other than the agent of the principal who signed the permission.
   "bypass":  Never go around an access control, a block, or a refusal.
   "escalate":  Never obtain or use access that the agent was not given.
   "conceal":  Never hide, alter, or leave out the agent's own records.
   "deceive":  Never state what the agent has reason to believe is
      false, and never make up a record or a result.
   "harass":  Never threaten, pressure, or attack a person.
   "disclose":  Never pass information that is not public to anyone the
      permission does not name.
   "continue-after-stop":  Never carry on after being told to stop.

Appendix D.  Changes Since -00

   This section is to be removed before publishing as an RFC.

   *  The content model (Section 4 through Section 10) is specified
      apart from its encoding.  The JWS form is now Section 11, with its
      rules unchanged; Section 12 states what another encoding, such as
      one with COSE, has to define.  Log "lines" are now "entries"
      outside Section 11, and the codes in Appendix A are grouped by
      where they arise.

   *  No record's bytes change, but a verifier of -01 refuses some
      records that -00 accepted, to match versions 0.2.0 to 0.5.0 of the
      implementation.  From version 0.2.0: an Ed25519 key that encodes a
      y of p or more, or the y of a point of small order, is refused as
      a key wherever it stands, used or not (bad-key; in a TSA's
      certificate, stamp-invalid), where -00 refused only a signature
      verified under such a key; an "origin" outside the exact rule of
      Section 5, which needs no parser of web addresses; a TSA's RSA
      public exponent that is not odd, from 3 to 2^32 - 1 (stamp-
      invalid); and a TSA certificate's public key not written strictly
      (stamp-bad-data).  From version 0.4.0: an Ed25519, ML-DSA-87, or
      SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s signature whose length is not its method's is a
      problem on every verifier, whether or not it implements the method
      (signature-invalid; Section 4.5), where -00 left it unverified on
      a verifier without the method.  From version 0.5.0: a document
      name that begins "provared." in a receipt's or an approval's
      "details" MUST be one of the seven reserved names, and only the
      three identifiers among them may be named more than once in one
      record (bad-field; Section 6), where -00 read every name as a
      label.

   *  Section 1 names more of what other drafts already have.
      Section 13 compares the two EMILIA Protocol drafts and maps the
      records onto the AUDIT architecture's record types.  Section 18
      says which records can show that a receipt was never written.

   *  Section 17 describes version 0.5.0 and a second implementation, in
      Python, by the same author.
